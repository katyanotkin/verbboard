## 2026-09-24 — "Do we really need a Hebrew UI?" (choose between options)
- Proposed: marketing lead, product manager (director), restraint/skeptic, buildable-in-this-codebase engineer, end-user advocate. User named marketing + PM; extras added by the skill (skeptic, engineer, advocate). User removed: none.
- Style: swarm -> director-led, 1 round — fit well (5 seats in parallel, synthesis by the caller).
- Rounds: 1 (cap 1). Stop reason: bar met.
- Unanimity in R1: unanimous (all five: keep-frozen middle path, no seat said drop or invest).
- Steelman: ran, opposing view was DROP (engineer: Hebrew as a translation target is the biggest recurring cost, ~6-8h to drop, revivable in ~2h; only ~39 of ~407 engaged sessions use he UI) — retained "freeze, don't drop" because the data is bot-inflated and labelling started only 2026-09-24, and dropping the UI silently drops Hebrew glosses for Hebrew-speaking learners of other languages unless a separate translation-language setting exists. Revisit trigger recorded.
- Recurring: engagement lift is a "localized UI" effect (es/ru/he all 29-39% vs en 8%), not Hebrew-specific; selection bias in every UI-language engagement rate.
- Note: separate "study language" from "UI language" in every product discussion here; the two are easily conflated (Hebrew is both).
- Owner reaction (2026-09-25): rejected the "freeze / per-key English fallback" middle path: "we either remove it or keep it fully going, no value in partial UI". Next time, do not propose partially-maintained UI options for a user-facing language: frame the choice as fully maintained vs removed (with the study-vs-UI-language and translation-gloss consequences), and put the deferral in the decision date, not in a degraded product.

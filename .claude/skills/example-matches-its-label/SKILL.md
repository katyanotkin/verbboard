# An example must be what its label says

Owner rule (2026-11-05, English review): the generator produced "Mean it." and a "Don't say sorry unless you
mean it." style sentence as imperatives; the second is a present-tense sentence, not a command. The owner asked
to "make a rule of checking for such things".

## Steps

1. For every example you write or review, ask: does this sentence really use the labelled form in its labelled
   function? Imperative = a real command or request addressed to someone ("Stop hitting your brother!"),
   not a present/base-form sentence, not a bare two-word fragment ("Seem calm.", "Mean it.").
2. If the verb has no natural command (stative: seem, mean, need, want...), write NO imperative example; use
   another tense instead. Never invent one to meet a distribution.
3. The same applies to every other label: a "past" example must be past in meaning (not a present sentence with
   a past-looking form), a "conditional" example must express a conditional, etc.
4. When briefing agents (see `agreed-design-brief`) copy this rule in, and ask them to list sentences they
   rejected under it. When reviewing a batch, spot-check imperatives first: they are the most common offenders.
5. Generator prompts (`core/settings_ai.py`) carry the rule; keep them and this skill in sync.
